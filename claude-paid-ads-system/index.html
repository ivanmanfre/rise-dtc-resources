The 18 playbooks

One playbook per stage

From the first customer interview to the monthly profit report. Here is what each stage decides.

01

Market & Customer Research

Decides: who actually buys from you, why they buy, the words they use, and which customer group is worth paying the most to acquire.

02

Customer Psychology

Decides: what makes your best customers start looking, what they want to feel afterwards, what makes them hesitate, and the order in which those hesitations need answering.

03

Competitor Ad Analysis

Decides: what your buyers already hear from other brands, which angles are crowded, which are empty, where your price and terms sit, and which claims you must not copy.

04

Offer & Positioning

Decides: what you sell, at what price, with what bundle, guarantee and shipping terms, and the one sentence that says why a buyer should pick you over the alternative.

05

Angle Development

Decides: which three to five angles get briefed this month.

06

Hook Writing

Decides: the first line, or first three seconds, of each ad, and which openings get tested against each other.

07

Creative Briefs

Decides: what gets made this week, by whom, in what format, and exactly what each piece may and may not say.

08

UGC & Static Ads

Decides: which formats each angle ships in, how they get made, and how much each usable ad costs to produce.

09

Ad Copywriting

Decides: the primary text, headlines and descriptions around each creative on Meta, and the responsive search ad assets on Google.

10

Landing Page & CRO

Decides: where each ad sends traffic, what the first screen says, which objections the page answers and in what order, and whether a page change is worth testing at your traffic.

11

Campaign Structure

Decides: how many campaigns and ad sets your budget can support, what goes in each, how test money is kept apart from proven money, and how everything is named.

12

Meta & Google Build

Decides: the settings every campaign launches with: tracking, conversion event, attribution setting, budgets, audiences, bid strategy, ads and links.

13

Testing Framework

Decides: what gets tested, with how much money, by what rule a variant wins, loses or gets more time, and how results are recorded so they add up.

14

Retargeting Journeys

Decides: who gets retargeted, for how long, with which message, and whether the retargeting spend adds orders you would not have had anyway.

15

Budget & Bid Strategy

Decides: next month's ad budget, how it splits across campaigns and tests, which bid strategy each campaign uses and at what target, and when cash or stock should cap spend.

16

Scaling Playbook

Decides: when to add spend, how much at a time, how long to hold each step, what puts the budget back, and when to stop stepping and look for new places to spend.

17

Creative Refresh

Decides: when a winning ad is tired, what to change in it, and how many new versions your team can make each week.

18

Margin & Profit Reporting

Decides: how much money the ads left behind after paying for themselves, whether that is rising or falling, where the platforms' numbers and your store's numbers disagree, and what happens next week and next month.

How to install

Claude.ai or Claude Code

Download the zip and unzip it. Then pick where you run Claude.

›Download the full system

Claude.ai, any plan with Projects

  1. Create a Project, for example "Paid ads".
  2. Open a skill's SKILL.md, copy the whole file and paste it into the Project instructions. Or upload several SKILL.md files as Project knowledge.
  3. In a chat inside the Project, ask for the job by name ("run break-even-and-affordable-cpa on these numbers") and paste your data under it.

No setup: paste any SKILL.md into a new chat, paste your numbers under it, and send.

Prompts: copy a prompt from its category file, replace every [BRACKETED] input, and send. Upload the prompts/ files as Project knowledge to call them by number ("run P40 with these figures").

Playbooks: upload the playbooks/ files as Project knowledge, and Claude can walk you through a stage step by step.

Claude Code

Skills and agents install into ~/.claude/skills/. Run these from the folder where you unzipped the kit:

cp -R dtc-claude-skills/* ~/.claude/skills/
cp -R the-dtc-growth-team/track/* ~/.claude/skills/
cp -R the-dtc-growth-team/spend/* ~/.claude/skills/
cp -R the-dtc-growth-team/creative/* ~/.claude/skills/
cp -R the-dtc-growth-team/convert/* ~/.claude/skills/
cp -R the-dtc-growth-team/decide/* ~/.claude/skills/

Always use the cp -R <dir>/* form above. Never use cp -r <dir>/*/ with a trailing slash: on macOS that copies the contents of each folder instead of the folder, so every SKILL.md lands on top of the last one in a single flat pile.

Check it worked: ls ~/.claude/skills/ should list 45 new folders, each with its own SKILL.md. Claude Code picks them up on the next start.

The one rule everything follows

No invented numbers

Every skill, agent and prompt uses only what you paste and shows its arithmetic so you can redo it by hand. The worked examples in the playbooks use made-up numbers, labelled as examples. Replace them with your own.

  • It does not connect to anything

    Meta, Google, Shopify: none of them. You paste, Claude reads.

  • It does not predict your CPA or ROAS

    It shows what happened and what a change has to clear.

  • It does not fill gaps with averages

    A missing input prints [NEEDS: the missing input] in place of a benchmark.
