01 / The first pack

Does the first purchase need to be the stock-up?

A recorded travel ad leads to the 60-count pack at $29.99. The same product offers 21 brushes for $14.99. That gives GuruNanda a specific acquisition test, without a new product or discount.

Test the pack.
Keep the message.

The travel message gives us the reason for the test: someone who wants a compact option for a trip may respond differently to a smaller pack. That is a hypothesis, not evidence that the 60-pack underperforms.

Keep the ad, audience and page content the same. Split eligible new-customer traffic between the existing 60-count link and the 21-count variant. Check the selected pack on mobile before either version goes live.

More purchases are useful only if the economics hold.

At $14.99 versus $29.99, the smaller pack needs approximately twice the conversion rate to match product revenue per click. That is not the profit threshold. Product cost, shipping income and expense, discounts, fees and refunds can change the result. Compare contribution per paid click after those costs, then subtract the ad cost.

Sources: recorded travel ad (12 Oct) · exact 60-count destination · 21-count alternative (store checked 18 Oct).

See the ad-to-page evidence and test decision

The recorded ad opens with: “Traveling with a full-size toothbrush and toothpaste? Make it easier.” It does not promise a 21-count pack.

The destination query selects variant 51806984503525, the 60-count pack. The words “21-count” in the page address do not determine the selected variant. Product data also lists a 3-count pack at $2.25. We would keep that out of the first comparison until its shipping and acquisition economics justify a separate test.

Use actual orders to compare new-customer conversion, net order revenue and contribution per paid click. Keep returning customers separate. Set a decision threshold from current conversion volume and the smallest worthwhile profit difference before launch. If the 60-pack wins after costs, keep it as the entry offer.

02 / The add-on decision

A useful refill could take the kit past $50.

The Lion & Lamb kit costs $43.99. Its page already recommends compatible replacement brush heads at $9.99. Together they reach $53.98, above the published US free-shipping threshold.

The opportunity is placement.

The cross-sell exists in the page's lower recommendations. Test a compact, optional kit-plus-refill choice beside the main purchase control, with the combined price and shipping eligibility visible before the cart.

The refill needs its own reason.

The kit already includes brush heads. Present the add-on as replacements for later use, not something required to make the kit work. Keep the kit-only purchase easy to select.

Check the shipping rule and the margin trade-off

The detailed policy says retail orders over $50 qualify before taxes and/or discounts, with location and customer restrictions. Confirm the final cart qualifies after discounts. The current public product prices put this proposed combination $3.98 above $50.

A larger cart is not automatically a better order. Compare completed-order conversion and contribution per visitor, including the refill cost and any shipping subsidy. This proposal does not establish an existing bundle, an existing checkout defect or a forecast increase.

Sources: recorded kit ad (12 Oct) · kit destination and existing recommendations · matching refill · shipping policy (18 Oct).

03 / A creative worth separating

Show the pen before the whole routine.

The recorded whitening ad focuses on getting ready while using the kit. The product page also describes a mess-free gel pen. That gives us a second, more specific product demonstration to test.

Recorded ad message · 12 October

“Hair in Progress. Smile in Progress.”

The current idea sells a moment in the day. The proposed idea shows how one part of the kit works. Keep the $24.99 offer and destination unchanged so the first test answers a creative question.

Open

Show the actual pen and its applicator in close-up. Make the product action clear in the first shot.

Show

Film its use according to the product instructions. Then reveal the rest of the kit, with each item named on screen.

Close

Show the full kit and current price. Lead to the existing product page rather than introducing a separate offer.

Proposed filming brief, not an existing ad. Use real footage and approved product claims. No invented whitening result or before-and-after transformation.

What would make this creative worth keeping?

Compare purchase cost and contribution after advertising, with delivery, audience and offer held comparable. Watch-time and clicks help diagnose the creative, but neither proves profitable sales. The page already explains the pen, so this is a test of what the ad leads with, not a claim that the product information is missing.

Recorded whitening ad (12 Oct) · exact destination and kit contents (18 Oct).

04 / Make the kit easier to understand

Five products need a clear role.

The Shine Bright Stay Fresh ad leads with 30% off and several product types. The $27.99 page's FAQ gives buyers more context about how those products fit into a routine.

Use that existing explanation to build an alternative ad. Show the kit item by item, with a plain label for each product's role. Where products are alternatives, say so. Do not imply that every bottle belongs in every use.

Proposed comparison: the recorded discount-led message versus a product-role demonstration. Keep the price, discount and destination the same. Then compare new-customer purchase cost and contribution, not just video views.

Recorded kit ad (12 Oct) · current kit page and FAQ (18 Oct). This is a creative proposal, not a finding that the routine instructions are absent.

One link check before more spend

A recorded tracking URL contains two question marks.

The Shine Bright ad record puts ?utm_source=facebook inside the variant value. That prevents a standard URL parser from reading a separate utm_source parameter.

Recorded: ?variant=46383210070245?utm_source=facebook
Corrected form: ?variant=46383210070245&utm_source=facebook

Check the current account first. If the same URL remains, correct that separator, preserve the other parameters, and check the selected product plus analytics. The kit has one listed variant. This record does not prove a broken purchase flow or lost revenue.
