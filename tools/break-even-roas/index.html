  • ROAS is measured on gross, reported revenue, because that is the number the ad platform shows you and the number this page has to be comparable against.
  • Returned orders remove the whole order value from contribution, while return handling is charged on every expected return.
  • Ad spend is never refunded, so a returned order still carries the acquisition cost that bought it.
  • Contribution margin, return cost and your target margin stay fixed in the range below. Those are yours to set, not estimates on the return rate.
  • When contribution per order clears zero or below, break-even ROAS is undefined and the page says so instead of printing a number that implies a ROAS could still fix it.

Rounding happens at display time only. All the arithmetic runs in full precision, so nothing drifts between the panel, the spread and the rows above.

How to read it

Why returns raise the ROAS you need, beyond the return rate you report

Break-even ROAS answers one question: how much revenue does a dollar of ad spend need to buy before the order it bought stops losing money. Most founders answer it with a margin number alone and divide 1 by that margin. At the 45% margin loaded into the fields above, 1 divided by 0.45 gives 2.22x, which is the naive row printed in the math card. That number is real, but it is missing the return that has not happened yet at the moment the ad platform reports the sale.

ROAS is measured on gross revenue, on purpose

Meta, TikTok and every ad platform report return on ad spend against the gross value of the order at the moment it is placed, before a refund can happen. That is the number sitting in your ads dashboard, and agencies often quote a flat 4x target to hold it against, which is why 4x is loaded into the reported-ROAS field on this page and then tested against your own margin instead of assumed. This calculator keeps that same denominator. Break-even ROAS here is gross order value divided by contribution per order, so the answer it hands back is directly comparable to the number the platform already shows you.

Where returns enter a margin that already looks complete

Contribution margin at full price is usually quoted on an order that ships and stays sold: product cost out, fulfilment cost out, nothing else has touched it. Returns have not entered that number anywhere yet. This page adds them back in twice, because they cost you twice. First, the revenue from a returned order is gone, so only the kept share of orders contributes anything at all. Second, someone has to receive the parcel, inspect it and repack it, and that cost lands on every order shipped in proportion to how often orders come back. Both numbers pull contribution per order down, and since ROAS is 1 divided by contribution per gross dollar, pulling the numerator down pushes the required ROAS up.

Why 4x reads as safe and rarely is

Triple Whale's 2025 apparel and accessories medians, quoted in full in the panel above, sit in a narrow band: 2.18x on Meta and 2.49x on TikTok. A flat 4x target sounds comfortably above that band, which is exactly why it gets repeated. But those medians describe what the platforms are delivering across a category, while your own order sets its own bar. Run the example loaded into the fields above, a 45% full-price margin with a 22% return rate, and the panel asks for about 3.2x just to clear zero, and considerably more than that to leave a real net margin once ad spend is paid. A platform-reported ROAS that beats the category median can still sit below your own break-even line, and the only way to know is to run your own margin and your own return rate through the math instead of borrowing someone else's average.

What target ROAS actually asks for

Break-even ROAS only gets you to zero. Target ROAS on this page solves for the ROAS that leaves the net margin you actually want after ad spend, which is a stricter number and usually the one that belongs on a media plan instead of the break-even figure. When contribution per gross dollar cannot clear the target margin at any spend level, this page says so directly rather than printing a ROAS nothing could reach, because at that point the constraint sits in the order rather than the ad account.

When no ROAS works

If contribution per order is at or below zero before a single ad dollar is spent, no ROAS a platform could report repays it. No amount of creative testing or audience refinement fixes that. It is a signal to look at the return rate, the return cost or the margin first, the same three dials this page asks for, before spending more to acquire orders that were already losing money on arrival.

The range on this page flexes only the return rate, since that is the number most founders are estimating rather than reading off a settled report. Contribution margin, return cost and the target margin are choices you make rather than guesses about the world, so they stay fixed while the return rate moves 20% in each direction. A best case that still asks for an uncomfortable ROAS is telling you the fix sits in the order itself, before the next round of testing.

Questions we get

Six things founders ask about this math

Why is ROAS measured on gross revenue instead of revenue after returns?
Because that is what the ad platform reports and what you are actually comparing your number against. Meta, TikTok and every other platform quote ROAS on the gross order value the pixel fires on, before any refund happens. This calculator keeps that same denominator so the break-even number it hands back is directly comparable to the number already sitting in your ads dashboard.
Why does a higher return rate raise the ROAS you need, instead of just lowering revenue?
A returned order removes its revenue from your contribution, but the ad spend that bought it is never refunded. Contribution margin is the numerator of break-even ROAS and revenue is the denominator, so when returns shrink the numerator without touching the denominator, the ROAS required to clear zero goes up. That is the whole mechanism behind the returns penalty printed on this page.
Is 4x really a safe target for ROAS?
Only if your margin, once returns are inside it, clears 25 cents of contribution per gross dollar. Triple Whale's 2025 apparel medians, quoted with their link in the published figures panel on this page, sit at 2.18x on Meta and 2.49x on TikTok, both under the flat 4x target agencies tend to quote. At the example loaded into the fields above, a 45% margin with a 22% return rate still asks for more than 4x before a meaningful net margin appears. The 4x figure is a media buyer's shorthand, and this page tests it against your own numbers rather than assuming it.
My contribution margin already includes everything, so why do I need a separate return cost input?
Contribution margin at full price is quoted on an order that ships and stays sold. It has no return in it yet. This calculator adds the return back in twice: once by removing the revenue on the returned share, and once by charging the label, inspection and repack cost you enter here on that same share. Folding both into one margin number would hide exactly the two places returns cost you.
What does it mean if no ROAS breaks even?
It means the order loses money before you spend a single dollar on acquisition, at any ROAS a platform could report. Fix the order first, either the margin, the return rate or the return cost, before you spend more on the ads that buy it. No amount of ROAS discipline repairs unit economics that are already underwater.
How is the range calculated, and why call it best case and worst case instead of low and high?
Return rate is the number most founders are estimating rather than reading off a report, so this page recomputes break-even ROAS with it flexed 20% higher and 20% lower. A lower ROAS requirement is the good outcome here, so calling that case best case and the higher requirement worst case keeps the labels honest. Low and high would read backwards, since a lower number is the one you want.
