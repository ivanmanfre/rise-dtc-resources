    How to read it

    The five things this grader looks at

    A subject line has one job in a crowded inbox. It has to tell a person who is scanning a full screen of them what is inside, give them a reason to care about it today, and do that inside the number of characters their phone will actually show. The five marks below are the parts of that job, weighted by how much each one moves whether the message gets opened.

    Clarity, weighted 2.5

    Can a reader tell what the email contains from the line alone, in one pass, without hovering for a preview. This carries the heaviest weight because a line nobody understands never gets the chance to be interesting. Clarity is where restock lines, order lines and shipping lines win by default and where clever wordplay usually loses.

    Curiosity, weighted 2.0

    Does the line give a reason to open it today. What scores here is a specific promise the reader has not already seen from you this week. A cliffhanger and a withheld noun both cost clarity, and the grader gives nothing back here for that trade. A line that says exactly what is inside and makes that thing sound worth opening scores well on clarity and curiosity at the same time, which is the combination that actually gets sent.

    Specificity, weighted 2.0

    The grader is looking for a name where a category would have done: a product name, a size, a colour, a number of days, a named collection. Specificity is what separates a line written for one list from a line that could have gone to any brand in the vertical, and it is the mark that improves fastest once you go looking for it.

    Spam risk, weighted 2.0

    Scored so that ten is safe and zero reads like a promotion nobody asked for. Shouting capitals, stacked exclamation marks, currency shouting like $$$ or 100% FREE, and the phrases that turn up in every discount blast all pull this down. A low mark here caps the verdict no matter how the rest scores, because a line that trips a reader's spam instinct never reaches the part where the offer matters.

    Length fit, weighted 1.5

    Whether the line survives the truncation of a phone inbox with the meaning intact. This is the lightest weight for a reason. The bands are a working rule, and where the front of the line carries the point, a long line can still land. What the mark actually measures is whether the important half arrives before the cut.

    Questions we get

    Five things people ask about the grader

    Why does this one ask for an email when the other tools do not?
    Twenty-three of the twenty-five calculators run in your browser, so they cost us nothing and require nothing to see the answer. This one sends your line to a model on our side and pays for the answer, so it is capped at five runs a day per address and the address is how the cap is counted. Everything the page explains about the five marks is readable without the form.
    What happens if I hit the daily cap?
    The page tells you, in a sentence, and it tells you when the runs come back. There is a per-address cap of five runs a day and a whole-site cap so the tool cannot run up a bill overnight. Nothing is queued and nothing is charged. Come back the next day, or send the line you have and grade the winner later.
    Is the score the same if I run the same line twice?
    It should be. The grader runs at a low temperature precisely so the same line comes back with the same marks, which is what makes two lines comparable to each other. The three rewrites can vary slightly between runs. If you get two clearly different scores for the same line, the line is sitting on a band edge and the honest read is that it is somewhere in the middle.
    Can I paste a whole email in there?
    No, and the field will tell you so. The grader takes one line of up to 200 characters and rejects anything with a line break in it. If you paste a body the marks stop meaning anything, because clarity and length fit are both measured against what a phone inbox shows for a subject.
    What do I do with a line that scores badly?
    Read which of the five marks came back lowest, since that is the one the three rewrites are aimed at. Then send the rewrite against your current line to a slice of the list and let the open rate settle it. If the two lines are close, the CTR and A/B significance tool tells you whether the gap you are looking at is real yet.
