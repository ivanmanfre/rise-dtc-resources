    The angles

    What each of the six is for

    The order is fixed and every run returns all six, so two runs of the same product are comparable card for card. Each angle has a job, and each one has a thing it reaches for when the description runs thin, which is the thing the generator is built to refuse.

    Problem-first

    Opens on the friction the buyer already feels, in words a person could say out loud, then puts the product where that friction was. It has no survey, so it stays quiet about how common the problem is.

    Proof-shaped

    Carries the shape of social proof with none of its substance: who the thing is built for, when it gets used, what standard you said it was built to. Review counts, star ratings, customer counts and testimonials are all barred from this angle, because the generator has none of them.

    Offer-led

    Leads on your offer, restated in your own terms. With the offer field empty it leads on what the buyer gets for their money and carries no urgency of any kind.

    Sensory

    Takes one physical detail out of your description, the weight or the material or the fit, and carries it the whole way through. It works with the detail you gave it and stops there.

    Objection-flip

    Names the reason this particular buyer hesitates, then answers it with a fact from your description. The answer is a plain assertion, and it stays away from returns windows and warranties you never mentioned.

    Direct

    The plainest statement of what this is, who it is for and what to do next, with no hook mechanics at all. It is the control variant, and it is the one the other five have to beat.

    There is no benchmark table on this page. No source we can fetch states how these six angles perform against each other at the standard the rest of these tools hold, so the page prints none. What the tool gives you is six honest starting points and a test you can run this week.

    How to read it

    The rules this generator writes under

    An ad in a feed is competing with a photograph of somebody's dog, and the first line is the whole fight. This page writes six versions of that fight from four fields. The rules below are the reason the output is safe to put on a client's ad account, and the server enforces every one of them on the copy after it comes back.

    The four fields are the only source

    The product name, the description, the audience and the offer are all the generator gets. It has no access to your reviews, your ratings, your press, your stock levels, your shipping terms or your returns policy, so it writes none of them. A number that shows up in one of the six ads appeared in one of your four fields first, and the server rejects the response when it did not.

    An empty offer field means no urgency at all

    Urgency is licensed by one field. Fill the offer in and the offer-led variant restates your terms in your own words, deadline included if you gave one. Leave it empty and no variant carries a deadline, a discount, a countdown or a stock warning, because there is nothing to be urgent about. That single rule is what keeps "only 3 left" off an ad account that has plenty in stock.

    The 80 character hook rule is ours

    Every hook comes back at 80 characters or fewer. That is this tool's own working rule, set against the roughly 125 characters a phone shows before the See More link, with room left for the brand name sitting above it. It is the rule we use on client accounts, and the page labels it as ours.

    Health and body claims come back softened

    A description that says clinically proven comes back as copy about the person and the routine. The generator declines to claim an effect on a human body, and that holds even when your own description makes the claim. If yours is substantiated, put it into your ad manager yourself. This page has no way to verify a substantiation, and the liability sits with whoever published the sentence.

    Voice rules, enforced after the fact

    Em dashes, exclamation points, the corrective contrast shapes, the round-number listicle openers and a long list of filler words are all checked on the way out. A response that trips one of them is rewritten once, and a second failure returns an error in place of the copy. The error is the safe failure mode here: a sentence that fails the check twice never reaches your ad account.

    One run writes six, and there is no regenerate button

    Six variants come back together and that spends one of your five runs for the day. There is no regenerate button, because every press would spend another one. If the six are wrong, the fix is almost always the description field: give it the material, the size and what changes for the person who owns the thing.

    Questions we get

    Five things people ask about the generator

    Why does this one ask for an email when the other tools do not?
    Twenty-three of the twenty-five calculators run in your browser, so they cost us nothing and require nothing to see the answer. This one sends your four fields to a model on our side and pays for the answer, so it is capped at five runs a day per address and the address is how the cap is counted. Every rule the generator writes under is readable on this page without filling the form in.
    What happens if I hit the daily cap?
    The page tells you in a sentence, and it tells you when the runs come back. There is a per-address cap of five runs a day and a whole-site cap so the tool cannot run up a bill overnight. Nothing is queued and nothing is charged. Come back tomorrow, or run the six you already have against each other in the meantime.
    Will I get the same six ads if I run the same product twice?
    No, and that is deliberate. This one runs warm, so the six angles come back written differently each time and a second run on the same product gives you six more to test. The rules are what stay fixed: same angle order, same length bands, same banned list, checked on the server every run.
    What if I leave the offer field empty?
    You still get all six variants, and the offer-led card leads on what the buyer gets for their money instead of a discount. With that field empty the whole response is scanned for percent signs, currency symbols, weekdays and month names, and a response carrying any of them is rejected and rewritten. An empty offer field is an honest state, and the six you get back are the ones you can run when nothing is on sale.
    Can I use it for a supplement or a skincare product?
    Yes, and the copy will read more carefully than what you typed. Words like cure, heals, clinically proven and reduces inflammation are barred from the output whatever your description says, and a named condition presented as something the product acts on is barred with them. What comes back is copy about the person, the format and the routine, which is the register that survives a Meta review.
