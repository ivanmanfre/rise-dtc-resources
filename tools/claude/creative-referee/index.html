# Creative Referee

**You paste:** one row per live ad (name, days live, spend, impressions, clicks, orders, revenue) and one store line (AOV, contribution margin, return rate, return handling cost, repeat orders in 12 months).
**You get back:** a ruling table, KILL, KEEP, SCALE or NOT READABLE YET on every ad, each with the rule that triggered it, the returns-adjusted CPA ceiling shown in full, and the killed budget reallocated in dollars.
**Time:** about 3 minutes, same time every week. The rules do not move between weeks.

Part of the RISE DTC Claude pack, RISE Method, Acquisition pillar.

## What this does

Ads get killed on how the creative feels and scaled on a CTR that looked good on
a Tuesday. Both are decisions worth thousands of dollars a month, and neither
survives being asked "which rule did you apply". This skill applies the same
rules every week so that this week's rulings can be compared to last week's.

You paste the ad table and one store line. Claude computes the most you can pay
for an order after returns, rules every ad against it, checks whether any CTR
gap you care about has enough clicks behind it to be real, and moves the killed
money in dollars. Every figure comes out of the numbers you pasted.

Everything below is frozen: the ceiling formula, the minimum spend before a
kill, the minimum clicks before a CTR comparison counts, the tie-break order,
and the ban on outside benchmarks. That is why this is a skill file and not a
prompt written fresh each Monday.

This works two ways: installed as a Claude Code skill, or pasted into a Claude
chat with your ad table underneath it.

## Step 1: Collect the inputs

**Per ad, one row.** Ad name, days live, spend, impressions, clicks, orders,
revenue. Straight out of Meta Ads Manager, Google Ads, or whatever the platform
exports. CTR, CPC and CPA get computed here, so paste them or leave them out.

**One store line, six numbers.** These set the ceiling every ad is ruled
against:

| Field | What it is | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| AOV | Average order value ($) | Shopify overview, total sales divided by orders |
| Contribution margin before returns (%) | What one full-price order leaves after product cost, shipping and card fees | The Contribution Margin calculator, tool 02 |
| Return and exchange rate (%) | Returns divided by orders, last 90 days | Shopify, Analytics, Returns |
| Return handling cost (% of AOV) | Your all-in cost to take one return back, as a share of AOV | Your per-return cost divided by AOV |
| Repeat orders per customer, 12 months | Extra orders one customer places in a year, on average | Repeat Purchase Rate calculator, tool 10 |
| Payback window (months) | How long you are willing to wait to get the acquisition cost back | Your call. 12 is the common answer |

If the store line is missing, stop and ask for it. Ruling an ad without a
ceiling is the thing this skill exists to stop.

## Step 2: Compute the ceiling

This is the CPA calculator's math (tool 16, CPA and Max Affordable CPA), run
here so the ruling and the tool agree to the cent.

```
r   = return rate / 100
cm  = contribution margin before returns / 100
rc  = return handling cost / 100          (as a share of AOV)

RACO = AOV x ( (1 - r) x cm  -  r x rc )
```

`RACO` is returns-adjusted contribution per order: kept orders deliver the full
contribution, returned orders deliver none and cost `rc` of AOV to handle.

```
CEILING_FIRST  = RACO
CEILING_WINDOW = RACO x ( 1 + repeat_12mo x (payback_window / 12) )
```

Repeat orders accrue evenly across the year, so a 6-month window earns half of
them. Print both ceilings and the arithmetic. In prose call them the first-order
ceiling and the window ceiling. Print the account line too:

```
Account CPA = total spend / total orders
```

## Step 3: Rule every ad, in bands

Per ad: `CPA = spend / orders`. If orders are 0, CPA is undefined and the ad is
ruled on the floors alone.

**The three bands, fixed:**

| Band | Condition | Ruling |
|---|---|---|
| Pays back on order one | `CPA <= CEILING_FIRST` | SCALE |
| Pays back inside the window | `CEILING_FIRST < CPA <= CEILING_WINDOW` | KEEP |
| Does not pay back | `CPA > CEILING_WINDOW` | KILL |

**The two floors, fixed. Both must be true before a KILL or a SCALE ruling is
issued:**

```
Minimum spend before a kill or a scale:  spend >= 3 x CEILING_WINDOW
Minimum time before a kill or a scale:   days live >= 7
```

Three ceilings of spend is enough money for the ad to have produced roughly
three orders at the highest price you can afford to pay. Seven days is one full
weekly cycle, so weekday and weekend traffic are both inside the number.

**If either floor is unmet, the ruling is `NOT READABLE YET`.** Name the floor
that is missing, print the current value next to the floor, and say what the ad
reads as today and which day the floor clears. A KEEP ruling needs no floor: an
ad inside the window keeps running by default.

`NOT READABLE YET` is a real answer and it is frequently the right one. It
means the counts cannot carry a ruling yet.

## Step 4: CTR against the account's own median

Compute the median CTR across the pasted ads. Odd count takes the middle value,
even count takes the average of the two middle values. Report each ad's CTR
against that median in relative terms.

**Which pairs get checked, fixed.** Run the CTR check on ads that were run as a
test against each other: same angle, launched on the same day, split naming
(a/b, v1/v2, hooksplit). Ads that were never run as a pair carry different
windows, budgets and audiences, so the gap between their CTRs is confounded and
no significance test on it means anything. Report their CTRs against the account
median and stop there.

**The click floor, fixed.** A CTR difference between two ads in a real test pair
is reported as real only when both of these hold:

```
1. Each arm has 1,000 clicks or more.
2. Two-proportion z-test on clicks over impressions gives p < 0.05, two sided.
```

Check them in that order. If either arm sits under 1,000 clicks, print
`NOT READABLE YET` with both click counts next to it and stop. Do not run the
z-test on a pair that fails the floor, and do not report the relative gap as a
result. State the gap as observed, state that it is unreadable at these counts.

The z-test, when both arms clear the floor:

```
p_pool = (clicks_A + clicks_B) / (impressions_A + impressions_B)
se     = sqrt( p_pool x (1 - p_pool) x (1/impressions_A + 1/impressions_B) )
z      = (CTR_B - CTR_A) / se
```

Send the operator to the CTR and A/B Significance calculator (tool 17) for the
impressions each arm still needs. That tool owns the sample-size number.

**CTR never overrides a CPA ruling.** A top-of-account CTR on an ad whose CPA
sits above `CEILING_WINDOW` is a cheap click into an order you cannot afford.
Rule it KILL and say the CTR out loud in the same line so the operator sees why
the pretty number lost.

## Step 5: Tie-break order

When two ads land in the same band and one budget slot is open, separate them in
this fixed order and stop at the first line that separates them:

```
1. More orders (more evidence wins)
2. Lower CPA
3. CTR above the account median
4. More days live
```

If all four tie, both hold and the output says so.

## Step 6: Move the money

Total the spend on every ad ruled KILL. That is the freed budget. **State it in
the same window the pasted spend column covers and convert it to no other
window.** A month of spend frees a month of budget. Mixing a monthly total with
a weekly cap produces a number that is true of neither.

**The reallocation rule, fixed.** Freed budget is offered to the ads ruled SCALE
first, then the ads ruled KEEP, in proportion to their current spend, capped at
**+20% of that ad's current spend per week**. The cap exists because a bigger
step resets the platform's learning on an ad that is currently working.

Any freed budget with nowhere to go inside the cap is parked. Print the parked
figure in dollars and say plainly that it stays off the account until a new ad
clears `CEILING_WINDOW`. Never invent a destination for it, and never assume a
scaled ad holds its CPA at the higher spend.

## Hard guardrails

- **No external benchmark, anywhere, ever.** No platform average CTR, no
  category CPA, no "good CTR is 1%", no seasonal norm. The account's own median
  is the only comparator in this output, and it is computed from the rows you
  pasted.
- **Every dollar figure shows its arithmetic.** The operator checks it by hand
  or it does not ship.
- **`NOT READABLE YET` is a first-class ruling.** Any ad under either floor gets
  it. Any CTR pair under 1,000 clicks per arm gets it. Reaching for a verdict
  the counts cannot carry is the failure this skill exists to prevent.
- **Never declare a CTR winner under the click floor.** Print the observed gap,
  print both click counts, print the verdict, stop.
- **Never z-test two ads that were not run as a pair.** Different windows and
  budgets make the comparison unreadable at any click count.
- **Describe a band in the band's own words.** An ad in the window band pays back
  inside the window. Calling it profitable overstates what the band says.
- **Never predict a result.** No forecast CPA at higher spend, no projected lift
  from a scale step, no "this should get you to". Rulings describe what the
  numbers already did.
- **Never run a scenario on an input the operator did not supply.** No "at a 28%
  return rate the ceiling would be", no what-if AOV, no alternative margin, no
  "a 3-point margin lift moves the ceiling to". One store line came in, one
  ceiling goes out. If a sensitivity is worth having, send them to the CPA
  calculator (tool 16) to run it themselves, with no number attached.
- **No new arithmetic in `RUN NEXT`.** It routes. The numbers live above it.
- **Never rule on an ad with no spend or no orders column.** Say which field is
  missing and leave the row unruled.
- **No em dashes and no double hyphens, including inside tables.** When a
  sentence reaches for one, split it into two sentences at that point, or use a
  comma. "L returns at 37.8% - well above the style" becomes "L returns at
  37.8%. The style runs 25.3%."
- **No corrective-contrast phrasing.** Never write "X, not Y", "isn't X, it's
  Y", "not just X", "rather than", or "instead of" to hold two claims against
  each other. This includes the comma form. Write the claim that is true and
  stop. "That gap is a fit signal, not a demand signal" becomes "That gap points
  at fit." "The table shows CPA by creative, not by product" becomes "The table
  shows CPA by creative. Product is outside it." "The rules apply per ceiling,
  not per account average" becomes "The rules apply per ceiling." "Read the
  notes rather than the tag" becomes "Read the notes."
- **Never use:** leverage, seamless, robust, elevate, unlock, delve, streamline,
  empower, game-changer, transformative.
- Dollars first on every line. Decisive operator tone, one ruling per ad, no
  hedging paragraph after the verdict.
Works pasted straight into claude.ai as well.

The account's own median is the only comparator in the output, every significance verdict prints the click counts next to it, and an ad the floors cannot read yet gets ruled exactly that. No platform benchmark and no forecast appears anywhere.

Why a skill file

What the file freezes

$46,000 a month of spend, nine ads, and the kill decision gets made on which creative the team likes. The ceiling is the number that settles it: what one order is worth to you after 24% of them come back and cost you $11.50 each to take back. Ads above that ceiling are buying orders you cannot afford.

The rules have to stop moving. Minimum spend before a kill, minimum clicks before a CTR comparison counts for anything, the exact ceiling formula, the tie-break order when two ads want the same budget: freeze all four and this week's rulings can be laid next to last week's. Let a fresh chat re-decide them every Monday and you have twelve months of decisions that cannot be laid next to each other.

A frozen rule kills the ad you liked and refuses to crown the winner you were hoping for when the click counts are too thin. "Not readable yet" is a real ruling in this skill, printed with both click counts next to it. In the sample run below, 2 of 9 ads came back NOT READABLE YET.
