The prompts

Four prompts, run in order, in one Claude chat

You paste your 12-week sketch and six numbers once, then run the four prompts in one Claude chat in this order, because each answer is the input to the next one. Every prompt is printed in full, and the copy button puts it on your clipboard.

01

Set the bar

Computes a full-price baseline per window AOV and the discounted contribution per order for every promo in your sketch.

You are auditing my Q4 promo calendar. Work only from the numbers I paste.

STANDING RULES for every line you write in this chat, from this message to the last one:
- Plain markdown only. Headings, tables and fenced code blocks. Write no LaTeX and no math
  delimiters.
- Zero em dashes. Split at the dash into two sentences, or use a comma.
- Write no "it's not X, it's Y" phrasing and no "X, not Y" pairs. State the claim directly.
- Quote no lift figure, no return rate, no discount depth and no seasonal pattern from any store
  other than mine. There is no typical BFCM lift in this chat and no category average. My own last
  year is the only comparator you have.

  plausible figure.
- Every dollar figure shows its arithmetic on the same line or directly under it.
- Carry every intermediate figure at full precision, five decimal places or better. Round only the
  figure you print. Print dollars to the cent and percentages to two decimal places. A rounded
  contribution per order feeds a wrong lift on every row below it.
- Put no dash between a heading and its qualifier. Use a comma.
- Never use these words: leverage, seamless, robust, elevate, unlock, delve, streamline, empower,
  game-changer, transformative.
- Dollars lead every line.

Step 1. Compute a full-price baseline contribution per order for every promo window, and print the
arithmetic for each:

  baseline contribution per order for this window
    = (this window's AOV x contribution margin %) x (1 - my base return rate)
    - (my base return rate x cost of one return)

Run this once per distinct window AOV. Where I gave a promo its own AOV, that promo's baseline runs
on that same AOV at my base return rate. A bundle window sells a bigger basket, and comparing a
bigger basket against a single-item baseline reads as a win that the arithmetic did not produce.
Print one line naming which promos share which baseline.

Step 2. For each promo in my sketch, compute the contribution per discounted order on that same
window AOV:

  discounted contribution per order
    = ((this window's AOV x contribution margin %) - (this window's AOV x discount %))
      x (1 - discounted return rate)
    - (discounted return rate x cost of one return)

Step 3. Handle a missing discounted return rate this exact way, and say so on the row: run the promo
at my base return rate, write [NEEDS: return rate on discounted orders for this promo], and add one
sentence stating that the real bar sits above what you printed by an amount neither of us knows.
Never estimate the uplift. Never say discounted orders return more often at any particular rate.

Step 4. Print one table with a row per promo:

  | promo | window | discount used | baseline contribution/order | discounted contribution/order |

Stop at that table. Compute no lift, rule on no overlap between windows, and propose no replacement
offer in this message. Prompts 2, 3 and 4 ask for those three things in that order.

02

The required lift, in percent and in orders

Turns each promo into the lift it must produce before it pays, in percent and in orders, and marks it PASS or FAIL against last year's actual.

Now compute what each promo has to produce before it pays.

For each promo:

  baseline contribution for the window = baseline orders x baseline contribution per order
  orders needed at the discount        = baseline contribution for the window / discounted contribution per order
  required lift %                      = (orders needed / baseline orders - 1) x 100
  required lift in orders              = orders needed - baseline orders

Then compute the same required lift a second time with returns switched off, using
(AOV x contribution margin %) on both sides and no return cost at all. Call that column "required
lift if returns did not exist". It shows how much of the bar my return rate is carrying.

Print one table, sorted by required lift, largest first:

  | promo | discount | required lift | required lift if returns did not exist | last year's actual | PASS or FAIL |

Prompt 3 ruled on the overlaps and the riders

Part 1. Overlap

Three pairs qualify. The user flagged two. One was missed and is listed first.

Missed pair: BFCM (27 Nov - 1 Dec, 30% off) and December (1 Dec - 10 Dec, 22.38% effective)

Both windows name Tue 1 Dec. BFCM runs through Dec 1 (5 days: Nov 27, 28, 29, 30, Dec 1). December opens Dec 1.

A customer on Dec 1 who holds both the BFCM 30% code and the December 15% code takes the BFCM code; the December gift does not close a 7.62-percentage-point gap.

Ruling: [NEEDS: does the BFCM code expire at the end of Nov 30 or the end of Dec 1?] If it expires end of Dec 1, December's opening day runs at 30% cost (BFCM rate), not 22.38%, and the first ~48 of December's 483 baseline orders generate margin at $12.68624 per order, not $18.49400. Flagged pair 1: BFCM (27 Nov - 1 Dec, 30% off) and Cyber Monday (30 Nov, 35% off)

Nov 30 sits inside the BFCM window. BFCM runs 30% through the same day CM runs 35%.

A customer arriving Nov 27, 28, or 29 at 30% off who knows Nov 30 brings 35% waits one to three days and buys on Cyber Monday instead.

Ruling: [NEEDS: daily order breakdown for last year's BFCM window, Nov 27, 28, 29, 30, Dec 1 separately.] The question to answer is whether Nov 27-29 daily orders ran below the 48.33 baseline while Nov 30 ran well above it. If so, last year's +152.5% BFCM lift and +152.1% CM lift are not independent measurements. Orders that migrated from Nov 27-29 to Nov 30 inflate the CM count and deflate the pre-CM BFCM count, and the two figures cannot be added as separate contributions to Q4.

Flagged pair 2: Cyber Monday (30 Nov, 35% off) and December (1 Dec - 10 Dec, 22.38% effective)

Dec 1 opens the day after the deepest day of the year closes. The step-down from 35% to 22.38% is 12.62 percentage points.

A customer who received Cyber Monday at 35% and did not buy sees Dec 1 open at 22.38% effective, a shallower offer than the one they passed on the previous day.

Ruling: [NEEDS: of last year's 585 December orders, what share came from customers who had also opened or clicked the Cyber Monday campaign?] That split determines whether December's +21.1% was driven by a fresh cohort (gift shoppers, late arrivals, customers outside the CM send) or by customers already price-anchored at 35%. The two populations face a different bar and the audit cannot separate them from the single lift figure supplied.
