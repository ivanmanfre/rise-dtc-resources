01 / Paid ads

Test a subscription-led offer line in your ads

Ten of the eleven ads we read offer 10% off a first purchase or order, while the product page offers 10% off for life on a subscription. Your returns page FAQ still answers "Do you offer subscriptions or discounts (student or NHS)?" with "Not at this time", so buyers meet three different answers.

One change

Keep the same video and product page. Change only the offer line: the new ad reads "Save 10% for life on a subscription. Pause, skip or cancel anytime." The current ad keeps its first-purchase line, and both run side by side. Update the returns FAQ first so it matches the subscription on sale.

02 / Paid ads

Test a customer coffee-stop scene against the doctor video

The ad leans on a doctor explaining the spray as convenient. A buyer review on the product page, from Aisha, describes a concrete moment: grabbing a coffee after workouts without showering first. That scene is not in the ad text sampled.

One change

Film a short scene of someone leaving a hot pilates class for a coffee, then one quick mist. Draft opening line: "Coffee straight after class? Mist, then go." Keep the offer line and product page unchanged, and run against the current ad. Get the reviewer's consent before reusing their story.

03 / Paid ads

Test a new video comparing Myokine Refresh with hypochlorous spray

The sampled video ad opens with a general curiosity question. Your product page already sets Myokine Refresh against hypochlorous acid spray in a comparison table, where your page states the alternative "Removes good and bad bacteria alike".

One change

Film a new 30-second video where Dr Jordan Faulkner walks through that table, and run it against the current video with the same offer line and product page. Draft opening line: "Using hypochlorous spray after training? Here is how Myokine Refresh differs." Check every comparison claim before running, and keep the "Refreshing scent" row out of the video, since the same page calls the spray "completely unscented".
