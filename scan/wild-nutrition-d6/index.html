01 / Paid ads

Add the breastfeeding limit to the motherhood ashwagandha ad

Your recorded motherhood ashwagandha ad sends people to a page that states the product is not suitable during pregnancy and breastfeeding. The ad text we read does not mention that limit, even though motherhood can include breastfeeding. We did not review the video itself.

One change

If the video does not already show it, add one line to the ad copy and on screen: 'Not for use in pregnancy or while breastfeeding.' Check the wording with your claims review. This is a safety fix to make now, not a test. If its targeting aims at new mothers, consider steering them to Pregnancy + New Mother Multi, which its page says suits breastfeeding.

02 / Paid ads

Test a small-dose answer in the collagen creator ad

Collagen 500 Plus is third in your best-selling sort with 621 reviews, and its page answers 'Why is your dose so low compared to many others?', so dose is a live question. Your brand ad lists '500 Da peptides', but the recorded text of both creator ads does not address dose; we did not review the video itself.

One change

Proposal, untested: duplicate the creator-style video ad and add an opening caption: 'Just 3g a day: 500 Dalton peptides. Smaller peptides, smarter formula.' Keep the original as control in a Meta A/B test, with equal budget, audience, page and dates. This is a message test only; check creator usage rights and your claims review first.

03 / Paid ads

Test the October-to-April advice on the Vitamin D ad

Your recorded Vitamin D ad promises seasonal support. Its page states that the UK Department of Health advises Vitamin D from October to April. The ad text we read does not give that reason, which is a concrete answer to why someone should start now.

One change

Duplicate the ad and open with: 'Shorter days are here. The UK Department of Health advises Vitamin D from October to April. One capsule a day.' Keep the original as control on the same page. Run both as a Meta A/B test with equal budget, audience and dates, each locked to one text. Show both only to UK audiences, since the advice is UK-specific. Check claims review first.

04 / Your store

Fix the conflicting £18 and £35 delivery thresholds

Your shipping policy says standard UK delivery is free for one-time orders over £18. It also charges £3.95 for one-time orders under £35. A shopper with a basket between those two figures cannot tell which rule applies, and product pages repeat the £18 figure.

One change

Confirm what checkout actually charges a one-time UK basket between £18 and £35. Then state that one rule in a single sentence on the policy page and the product pages. Do this before any ad promotes free delivery.

05 / Your store

Reconcile the 30-day guarantee with 14-day returns

Shoppers can't tell what happens if an opened jar isn't right for them. Product pages promise a 30-day happiness guarantee, while the returns page refunds within 14 days only for unused items in original packaging. These may be separate promises, but the pages do not say so.

One change

Confirm the real rule first, then make the product pages and the returns page state that same rule. A correction is not a test. Only if the 30-day promise already covers opened jars, test a clearer line such as 'Opened it and not right for you? Tell us within 30 days.' against the current wording.
