01 / Paid ads

Test price and store rating in the Stripe Polo ad

Your brown and cream Stripe Polo ads lead with style and occasion, and the recorded ad text names no price or rating. The polo page shows £50 and a 4.96 rating from 57 reviews, which is store-wide proof shown on other product pages too, so it could be tested in the ad itself.

One change

Keep the same image and the same polo page link, and test the current text against: "Stripe Polo in brown & cream, £50. Rated 4.96 across 57 Parmore store reviews." Only the ad wording changes, not the page it sends people to.

02 / Paid ads

Send the AW26 ad to a page of only its named styles

Your AW26 video ad names stripes, colourblock and camo, then sends people to Shop All, which lists all 19 products, from socks to caps and tees. A page showing only the two Stripe Polos, the Colourblock Swing Shirt and the Camo Quarter-Zip would carry just the styles the ad names.

One change

Keep the same AW26 video and text. Split its traffic at random between Shop All and a new page showing only the two Stripe Polos, the Colourblock Swing Shirt and the Camo Quarter-Zip. Only the landing page changes.

04 / Your store

Make the free UK returns promise match one policy

Product pages and the refund policy promise free UK returns, but your Returns & Exchanges page says refund postage is at the buyer's cost and only exchanges get a free label. Before ordering, a buyer cannot tell which promise applies.

One change

Decide which policy is current and make every page say it. If only exchanges are free, change the product page line to: "Free UK size exchanges within 14 days. Refund returns are posted at your cost."

05 / Your store

State the same £100 free delivery rule everywhere

Product pages promise free shipping on orders over £100, but the shipping page's free shipping answer only says cost is worked out at checkout. Two £50 Stripe Polos total exactly £100, so a buyer cannot tell whether that order qualifies.

One change

Fix this before promoting the offer: pick one wording, either "over £100" or "£100 or more", and use it on product pages and the shipping page. Draft answer: "Free UK delivery on orders of £100 or more. Other destinations are calculated at checkout."
