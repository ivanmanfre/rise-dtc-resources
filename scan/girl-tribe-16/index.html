01 / Paid ads

Match the Eras card ad price to the four-pack

Your Eras card ad says the card is $8, but the page it links to opens on the 4-card option at $32, with the single $8 card as another choice. The prices agree per card, yet a shopper expecting $8 sees $32 first, next to a pre-order note saying it ships December 1.

One change

Keep the same Eras page. Version A keeps the current $8 copy. Version B says: 'Get your whole mahj table playing: 4 Eras cards for $32, one for every player. Single cards $8.'

02 / Paid ads

Test a code-applied link on the homepage sale ad

Your homepage sale ads ask shoppers to type code EARLYBF26, while your holiday drop ad sends clicks through a link that already applies the code. Typing a code is one more step at checkout, so the two routes are worth comparing on the same ad.

One change

This is a route test. On the secret early Black Friday ad, version A keeps the homepage link and 'with code EARLYBF26'. Version B links through a code-applied homepage link and says 'your 20% is already applied from this link.'

03 / Proposed paid test

Film a friends gift swap for the Tini Time crew

Gift shoppers need to picture this crew landing well with someone else, and your Tini Time reviews describe that moment. A buyer named Allie says the crews were a hit as Christmas gifts with friends, and another buyer says one given as a gift was loved.

One change

Make one short video of friends at a holiday gift swap opening and trying on Tini Time crews, with the line 'The gift your girls will actually wear.' Run it against a product image ad for the same crew, both linking to the Tini Time page and showing the same order-by date for holiday delivery.

What we saw
Gift shoppers need to picture this crew landing well with someone else, and your Tini Time reviews describe that moment. A buyer named Allie says the crews were a hit as Christmas gifts with friends, and another buyer says one given as a gift was loved.

What to measure
