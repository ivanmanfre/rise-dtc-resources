01 / Paid ads

Test a filmed customer run-out story as the ad

The founder video is a review roundup ending in "start here", and the product page warns of "no hit on day one", so doubters need a reason to believe. Jakub Bucko, who puts little faith in supplements, ran out for about two weeks and saw the old patterns return; none of the 12 ads we read uses that moment.

One change

Film a short scene from Jakub Bucko's review, with their permission: a work desk, an empty Axolt box, then their words about running out, ideally spoken by Jakub on camera, or labelled a dramatisation if re-enacted. Label it one customer's experience, with the same page and call to action as the founder video.

02 / Paid ads

Test a coffee-swap ad that sets timing expectations

The coffee-swap ad promises "clear, steady focus through the afternoon", and its only timing cue is the guarantee's 24 days of daily use. The page it lands on says there is "no hit on day one" and to give it a full pack before judging; whether saying so in the ad changes first orders is untested.

One change

Run the current ad against a copy with one added line, same page, image and guarantee wording. Draft: "3pm used to mean the third coffee. Axolt is one sachet after lunch, no added caffeine. It builds over weeks, so give it one full pack." This is a message-only test.

03 / Paid ads

Test an HSA/FSA payment line in the study ad

The work-and-study ad sends people to the dollar product page. That page offers payment by HSA or FSA card through Truemed, but the ad does not mention this payment option, so it is worth testing whether naming it helps people decide.

One change

Keep the study ad as it is, and run a copy with one added closing line: "Have an HSA or FSA card? You may be able to pay with it at checkout. Eligibility is confirmed by Truemed." Use the same page, image and guarantee. This is a message-only test.

04 / Your store

Match homepage and FAQ pack terms to the product page

The homepage FAQ says each pack holds 30 sachets and arrives every 30 days, while the product page sells 24 sachets every 24 days. The FAQ page also asks for 30 days of use before a refund, against 24 days in the refund policy, so buyers comparing pages see two different deals.

One change

Fix today, no test needed: rewrite the homepage subscription answer and the FAQ guarantee answer so they state the same pack size, delivery interval and 24-day guarantee condition shown on the product page and refund policy. Also align the 3-month plan interval (72 days) and the ingredient count (46).

What we saw
