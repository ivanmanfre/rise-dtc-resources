01 / Paid ads

Fix conflicting stock signals on pages your shower ad links to

Your shower catalogue ad links to a Nuvo shower page saying expected into stock January 2027 and call before ordering, yet your feed marks every variant in stock. A top five best seller, the Polished Ceramic waste, shows an October 2026 stock date beside same-day dispatch, so either labels are stale or ads promote items you cannot ship yet.

One change

Confirm real stock for this shower, the Avant enclosure the same ad links to, and the Polished Ceramic waste. Where in stock, remove the stock date and call-first note, starting with pages the shower ad links to; where not, mark those variants out of stock in the feed so the catalogue ad stops showing them.

02 / Paid ads

Add free kerbside bath delivery to the baths ad text

A 178kg stone bath raises the question of how it will arrive, but the recorded baths catalogue ad text speaks only to design. Your pages offer free express pallet delivery on UK mainland orders, kerbside, usually within 3 days on a day your team arranges.

One change

Keep the ad's products and links and run a Meta A/B test on the text alone, excluding Northern Ireland and other non-mainland areas so everyone shown version B qualifies for the free mainland delivery it promises. Version B keeps the current text and adds: 'Free express delivery to UK mainland addresses, kerbside on a day agreed with you, usually within 3 days.'

03 / Paid ads

Show one sale-item returns rule on event pages

A sale buyer cannot tell which returns rule applies. The Kirkby bath page is marked SALE and offers 14 days to return or exchange, while your refunds policy says sale items cannot be returned, during an event promoted as up to 30% off.

One change

Decide the rule for discounted items, checking your legal obligations for online orders, then show it in the same words on sale product pages, the Haddon Event collection and the refunds policy.

04 / Proposed paid test

Test a stone-or-acrylic message in a new bath video

Choosing between stone and acrylic is a real bath decision. Your baths page states stone retains heat for longer and suggests acrylic when access is tight, and the 1700mm Bromley stone bath weighs 134kg against 46kg for the 1700mm Marlow acrylic.

One change

Film someone measuring a doorway and stair turn, then the two baths side by side, linking to your baths collection. Run the same footage twice: A with a design-only voiceover, B with 'Stone or acrylic? Stone holds heat for longer. Acrylic is lighter to carry in. Measure your route, then choose.'
