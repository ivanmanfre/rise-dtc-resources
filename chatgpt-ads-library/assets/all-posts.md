## A $35 ceiling in a worked example

A $100 order can leave you $35 to buy the customer.

That’s the HYPOTHETICAL example in the RISE DTC ChatGPT ads test kit. I’d run your own numbers before approving any spend.

Start with $100 in net sales, after discounts and expected refunds.

Product cost is $35, fulfillment is $9, payment fees are $3 and expected return handling is $3.

$100 − $35 − $9 − $3 − $3 = $50 before ads.

Keep $15 after acquisition, and the ceiling is $35. 💸

Can this example support a $45 CAC?

It doesn’t work at the $15 contribution target. You’d have $5 left for overhead and profit.

Setup and creative fees belong in the test budget too. Leaving them out creates a profit blindspot. Keep those one-off costs on a separate line.

The $3 return allowance covers handling. Expected refunded revenue is already deducted from net sales, so don’t subtract the same refunded dollars twice.

Replace every input with your store’s figures. I’d leave future repeat purchases out of this first-order calculation.

## Write the test decision before launch

The review date belongs in your ChatGPT ads test brief before the first dollar goes out.

I’d set the spending cap alongside it, with a rule for counting NEW customers. Give delayed orders and returns time to show up.

Say you spend $700 and attribute 20 first-time customers to the test. That’s a HYPOTHETICAL $35 acquisition cost.

Does that prove all 20 bought because of the ads?

It doesn’t work that way. Some of those people might have bought anyway.

In the RISE DTC test kit, record the acquisition cost beside what those orders left after product costs and fulfillment.

Include payment fees and return costs too. Leaving them out creates a profit blindspot in the review.

If tracking breaks, pause. If the page misses the offer, revise it.

When mature orders leave enough money after acquisition, you can approve another capped test.

Too few orders may leave the result unresolved. Give that outcome its own line on the review sheet. 👍
